<section id="reset" class="section reveal" data-reveal>
  <div class="section-head">
    <p class="eyebrow">Quit-first reset</p>
    <h2>Restore agency before optimizing effects</h2>
    <p>These steps are about reclaiming sleep, attention, and self-command — not winning a motivation contest.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="grid grid--steps">
    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">01</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Cut the easy path</h3>
      <p>Remove the frictionless exits: apps, saved links, repeat purchases, and the first-click ritual that keeps the loop alive.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">02</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Replace the cue</h3>
      <p>When the trigger hits, do a different sequence immediately: water, walk, music, journaling, or a sensory reset that does not deepen the loop.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">03</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Protect sleep</h3>
      <p>Anchor bedtime, reduce late-night screens, and treat sleep drift as the first visible cost of overuse.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">04</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Rebuild attention</h3>
      <p>Use short work blocks, daylight, movement, and protein-heavy meals to bring concentration back online without bargaining with cravings.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">05</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Recruit accountability</h3>
      <p>Tell one person who can notice drift before your internal story starts editing the evidence.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="step-card">
      <span class="step-card__number">06</span>
      <h3 class="step-card__title">Escalate support</h3>
      <p>If abstinence is harder than expected, get help that fits the problem: therapy, addiction care, or medical advice from a professional.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="risks" class="section reveal" data-reveal>
  <div class="section-head">
    <p class="eyebrow">Education / risk intelligence</p>
    <h2>What gets omitted when cannabis is over-simplified</h2>
    <p>The warning is not moral panic. It is respect for the ways a substance can shape attention, sleep, and the quality of your choices.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="grid grid--risks">
    <article class="risk-card">
      <span class="risk-card__label">Dependence</span>
      <h3>Routine can quietly become reliance</h3>
      <p>When the same substance gets tied to relief, transition, or escape, the habit stops being casual long before it stops feeling casual.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="risk-card">
      <span class="risk-card__label">Mood stability</span>
      <h3>Activation is not always relief</h3>
      <p>In vulnerable people, high-THC or low-buffer products can sharpen the edges that calmer use was supposed to soften.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="risk-card">
      <span class="risk-card__label">Judgment</span>
      <h3>Memory and focus move first</h3>
      <p>The brain’s story about itself often stays persuasive even after the underlying decision quality has already declined.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
